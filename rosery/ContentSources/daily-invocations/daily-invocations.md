## Waking up

Transfer him to a home better than his home (in the world), a family that is better than his (in the world), a wife better off than his (in the world) and let him enter Paradise and save him from the torment of the grave and the torment of Hell-fire.

**Practice summary**

### 96. The book’s Salatut Tasbih sequence

ID: `daily-salatut-tasbih` · Printed pages: 151, 152 · Footnotes: 60, 61

**Reading note:** The stage counts follow the photographed book. Its final stage is labeled “during At-Tashahhud”; that wording is preserved.

**Arabic**

سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ، وَالْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ، وَلَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَاللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ

**Source transliteration**

SubḥānAllāhi wal ḥamdulillāhi wa lā ilāha illāllāhu wallāhu Akbar

**Source translation**

Glory be to Allāh, praise is to Allāh, none has the right to be worshipped except Allāh and Allāh is Great

**Practice summary**

The book describes a voluntary prayer of four raka‘ah, with one or two taslims. In each raka‘ah it says to recite Al-Fatihah and a surah, then this formula 15 times while standing; 10 times in ruku‘; 10 after raising the head; 10 in the first sujud; 10 between the two prostrations; 10 in the second sujud; and 10 during what the book calls At-Tashahhud. It gives a total of 75 glorifications per raka‘ah and says to repeat the sequence in each raka‘ah until finishing.

**Recitation count:** Counts vary by prayer stage

- This is a compound prayer sequence: 15 at the first listed stage and 10 at each of the six following stages. A single recitation count would conceal the instructions, so the stage counts remain in the practice guidance.
- The book’s described method is retained as a source edition; its endnotes and independent evaluation of the narration are not included in these photographs.
